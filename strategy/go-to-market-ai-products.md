# Go-to-Market for AI Products

Launch and scale playbooks for AI-powered products.

---

## How AI GTM Differs

| Dimension | Traditional product GTM | AI product GTM |
|-----------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| **Expectations** | Set by category norms | Often set by first movers and hype |
| **Trust** | Features, brand, contracts | "Does it work?" + safety and reliability |
| **Education** | Feature benefits | What AI can/can’t do, and how to use it well |
| **Feedback loop** | Usage and support | Usage + quality, drift, and edge cases |

---

## Pre-Launch

### 1. Define the wedge

- **Single use case or persona** to win first (e.g. "developers doing code review" or "support agents answering FAQs").
- **Clear before/after:** What’s painful today, what’s better with your AI.
- **Success metric:** Adoption, time saved, quality, or business outcome you’ll track.

### 2. Set quality and safety bar

- **Minimum quality** for launch (e.g. accuracy, latency, refusal rate).
- **Safety:** Harmful output, bias, PII. Define red lines and monitoring.
- **Fallbacks:** What happens when the model fails or is uncertain (human handoff, fallback answer, clear "I don’t know").

### 3. Choose launch motion

| Motion | Best for | Typical first step |
|--------|----------|---------------------|
| **Closed beta** | High-stakes, enterprise, or sensitive use | Invite-only users, tight feedback loop |
| **Open beta** | Broad adoption, consumer, or dev tools | Waitlist or open signup with "beta" label |
| **Limited release** | Risk control or capacity | One segment, region, or tier first |
| **General availability** | Mature product, clear positioning | Full availability + support and SLA |

### 4. Prepare support and feedback

- **In-product feedback:** Thumbs, ratings, or "report issue" so you see failures and sentiment.
- **Support playbook:** How support triages AI issues (quality vs. bug vs. misuse).
- **Monitoring:** Quality and safety metrics, alerts, and a simple dashboard.

## Launch

### 1. Messaging

- **One-line value:** "[Who] can [do what] so that [outcome]." Example: "Developers ship faster with AI that suggests and explains code."
- **What it does and doesn’t do:** Set expectations to avoid "AI can do everything" and reduce disappointment.
- **Trust signals:** Accuracy, safety, privacy, or third-party evaluations where relevant.

### 2. Channels

- **Product-led:** In-app discovery, onboarding, and prompts to try the AI.
- **Content:** Use cases, best practices, and "how to get the most out of it."
- **Community / events:** Dev community, power users, or events to create pull.
- **Sales (if B2B):** Demos, pilots, and ROI stories for enterprise.

### 3. Early adopters

- **Who:** People with clear pain, tolerance for imperfection, and willingness to give feedback.
- **Ask:** Structured feedback (surveys, interviews) and permission to use quotes/case studies.
- **Retention:** Fix top issues fast; show that feedback drives product.

---

## Post-Launch: Growth and Iteration

### 1. Usage and quality loop

- **Track:** Adoption, frequency, completion, and quality/safety metrics.
- **Segment:** By use case, segment, or cohort to find where AI helps most and where it falls short.
- **Iterate:** Improve prompts, models, and UX based on data and feedback.

### 2. Expansion

- **Horizontal:** More use cases or workflows in the same product.
- **Vertical:** Deeper in one industry or role (e.g. "support for e‑commerce").
- **Audience:** Next persona or segment once the wedge is proven.

### 3. Monetization (if not at launch)

- **Free → paid:** Clear value moment before paywall; soft limits or usage caps to nudge upgrade.
- **Tiers:** Free / Pro / Team / Enterprise with clear limits and benefits.
- **Packaging:** Bundle AI with features that matter to the segment (see [AI Product Pricing Strategies](./ai-product-pricing-strategies.md)).

## Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| **Quality regressions** | Evals, regression tests, canaries, and rollback plan |
| **Safety incident** | Safety evals, monitoring, and incident playbook |
| **Overpromise** | Clear scope in messaging and docs; "known limitations" section |
| **Commoditization** | Differentiate on workflow, data, and outcomes, not just "we use an LLM" |

---

## GTM Checklist

- [ ] Wedge use case and success metric defined
- [ ] Quality and safety bar and monitoring in place
- [ ] Launch motion and first audience chosen
- [ ] One-line value and "what it doesn’t do" written
- [ ] Feedback and support paths ready
- [ ] Early adopter plan (who, how to recruit, what to learn)
- [ ] Post-launch review scheduled (e.g. 2–4 weeks)
